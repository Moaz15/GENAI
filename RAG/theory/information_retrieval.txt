1. What is a "Term"? 

(The Pre-Game)
Before a computer can count words, it needs to clean them up so it doesn’t get confused. 
This process is like "standardizing" an ingredient list.

Normalization: Turning everything to lowercase. To a computer, "Apple" and "apple" look like different things. We make them the same.
Tokenization: Chopping a sentence into individual pieces (usually words).
Stopword Removal: Deleting "fluff" words like the, is, and at. They don’t help you find specific info, so we toss them to save space.
Stemming/Lemmatization: Trimming words to their root.Example: "Running," "ran," and "runs" all just mean "run." This ensures that if you search for "run," you find all the related documents.

2. The Math: TF-IDF and BM25 (The Scoring)

Once the words are cleaned, we need to know how important they are. We use a few "stats" to decide this:
Term Frequency (TF): How many times does the word appear in this document? (More is usually better).
Document Frequency (DF): How many documents in the entire library contain this word?

The Logic: If a word appears a lot in one doc (High TF) but is very rare in the rest of the library (Low DF), it’s a goldmine for finding that specific document.

Calculating the Average
To keep things fair, we use a formula to make sure long documents don't "cheat" just because they have more words. We calculate the Average Document Length (avgdl):

avgdl = (1/N) * sum(dl(d))
(Where N is the total number of docs and dl is the length of a specific doc.)

Tokenization
In the world of AI and Search (Information Retrieval), tokenization is the first and most critical step. 
It is the process of breaking down a big "chunk" of text into smaller, manageable pieces called tokens.

1. How it Works
When you give a computer a sentence, it doesn't "read" it like we do. 
It sees a long string of characters. Tokenization chops that string based on specific rules.

The Basic Process:
Imagine the sentence: "The quick brown fox jumps!"

Splitting: The system looks for spaces.
["The", "quick", "brown", "fox", "jumps!"]

Cleaning (Normalization): It removes punctuation and makes everything lowercase.
["the", "quick", "brown", "fox", "jumps"]

Refining: It might remove "stop words" (like "the") because they don't help with search.
["quick", "brown", "fox", "jumps"]

2. Types of Tokenization
Depending on the goal (Simple search vs. Advanced AI like ChatGPT), different methods are used:

Word Tokenization: The simplest version. Every word is a token.
Issue: It struggles with "New York" (is it one word or two?) or complex words like "unbelievable."

Character Tokenization: Breaking text into every single letter.
Issue: It’s very slow because you end up with too many tokens.

Subword Tokenization (Used by Modern AI): This is the "Goldilocks" zone. It breaks words into meaningful chunks.
Example: "Smartest" becomes ["smart", "est"].

This allows the AI to understand that "smartest," "smartly," and "smarter" all share the same root.
